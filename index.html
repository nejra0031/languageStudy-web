Where your data lives

Your decks, your settings and the audio the dictation tab generates all stay on this device. Nothing is uploaded and there is no account. Saving starts immediately, in this browser's own storage — you need not set anything up. It is kept across reloads, but only for as long as this page's site data lasts.

Chrome and Edge can do better: pick a folder and the same files sit on disk where you can see them, copy them, and put them inside Drive or Dropbox for sync. Choosing a folder moves everything already saved into it; disconnecting moves it back. Firefox and Safari have no folder picker, so browser storage is the whole story there.

One file holds a whole setup: every deck and all your settings, ready to keep somewhere safe, carry to another machine, or bring back after a browser clears its storage. The API key is left out on purpose, and so is the dictation audio — it is a cache the Dictation tab refills, not something to mail around.

A backup is the whole store, audio and all: a zip laid out exactly like the data folder. It is the one copy that keeps the dictation bank, which cost real API calls to make. Unzip it anywhere and Chrome can open it as a data folder; or bring it straight back with Restore, which overwrites any file of the same name.

Gemini API key

needed for everything the model does

Dictation, Shadowing, Reading, Writing, Translate and Conversation call the Gemini API directly from this page with your key. It is stored in this browser's local storage, never alongside your decks, so it cannot end up in a git commit, and it is sent to nobody but Google. Get one free at aistudio.google.com/apikey.

Spends one text-model call from today's budget.

Models

Every Gemini model you use, listed once each. The limits belong to the model, not to the job it is doing — Google counts calls per model, so a model given two jobs below draws both of them from the one allowance you enter here, and typing it twice would only be a way of lying to yourself about what is left.

A model doing a job cannot be removed; give the job to something else first.

Calls / min and calls / day are counted in this browser before a request goes out, so you never discover a limit by being refused by Google. The windows roll over the last minute and the last 24 hours rather than resetting at midnight, so the day's budget trickles back an hour at a time. 0 means unlimited — the right answer for a paid key, and the one case where Google gets to be the one who says no. The defaults are the free tier.

What each model does

Pick the same model for two jobs and they share its allowance, exactly as they already do at Google's end. Speech is the one that really has to differ: only the TTS models return audio. Conversation suits a flash-lite model with a bigger daily allowance: one conversation spends five or six of its calls, each a line or two.

If a model ID is wrong the API's own error is shown verbatim, so a 404 here means exactly that. There is deliberately no hidden fallback list: the model that answers is the one you typed, and nothing quietly spends on another.

Language

feeds the prompts

The first note shapes the words the text model writes. The audio note is added to every request that makes audio with a Gemini voice, the dictation sentences and a Reading text read aloud, as a line of direction before the text. Your device's own voice and Azure voices do not use it.

The first three are for a dictation sentence. The writing words are how long an opinion piece on the Writing tab should be; a summary asks for about two thirds of that, more for a long text and less for a short one.

Any language the model writes is fine; type its English name. Answer checking is language-agnostic: it compares accents and diacritics exactly, and aligns words with them stripped, which works the same for Vietnamese tones, Spanish acutes and German umlauts.

Add from selected text

Select a word or a short phrase anywhere on the page, such as a new word in a dictation sentence, and a small form opens under it. It shows the word, its translation and an empty notes box, and Add puts the word into the deck below as a new card. If that deck already has the word, the form shows the card as it is stored and the button reads Update. Updating changes the meaning, the notes and whether the card is a grammar pattern, and keeps the card's score and history. Tick Grammar pattern for a construction such as hễ … là …; it ticks itself when the word has a … in it. You can select text in either language: text in your own language is taken as the meaning, and the word is looked up from it.

The translation comes from Google Translate, through the free public address its own website uses. It needs no key and spends nothing from your budget. It is also unofficial: Google can refuse it for a while, especially on a busy or shared network, and then the form says so and you type the meaning yourself. The selected text is sent to Google. Ask for notes is Gemini: it spends one call on the Notes model above and uses the notes prompt below.

Shadowing

A set is some lines to read aloud and record. Both sources are drawn from what you already have, and nothing on the Shadowing tab ever writes a new sentence — the only thing that does is the Dictation tab's New sentence button, and both tabs share one bank, so whatever it makes is immediately shadowable.

Each hand-in costs one call, however many lines are in it, because every recording you choose goes up together. You can hand in a whole set at once, or part of it now and the rest later, or just one retake. A set is whatever is actually available up to the number above, and the tab says so when it comes up short. The call is spent on whichever model the shadowing job is given above, out of that model's own allowance.

How the notes should be written, in your own words. A language (English), several (English and Vietnamese), or more than that (English, avoid technical terms, Vietnamese, with an English summary at the end). The words you are practising are always quoted as written. Left empty, the notes are in English.

What the feedback listens for, one rule per line, for the language above only — every language keeps its own. A line starting style: is about how the notes are written rather than what is heard. The rules are drafted for your language the first time you hand in a set, and each note says which ones it applied. Rate the notes on the Shadowing tab, and once enough notes under one version are rated, with at least one not useful, the rules are rewritten from what you said. Drafting and revising spend one call each on the text model. A line you write or change here is yours: revisions keep it word for word.

Shadowing prints the sentence in full, which a dictation deliberately hides. Sets prefer sentences you have already typed in Dictation, and Dictation prefers ones you have not yet shadowed, so the two tabs stay out of each other's way — but a sentence you shadow first is one whose dictation you have already read.

Removes every past set and every voice recording in them. The sentence bank is untouched.

Prompts

exactly what gets sent

Switch any prompt to Preview to see it as it will be sent: filled in with your settings and three of your cards. The preview cannot be edited; switch back to Edit to change the prompt.

Placeholders: {language} {level} {languageNote} {terms} {minWords} {maxWords}. The reply is read back from the TARGET: and EN: lines, so keep those two labels; everything else is yours to rewrite.

Placeholder: {sentence}. Bare {sentence} just reads it out. A prefix such as Read slowly and clearly for a language learner: steers the delivery. The answer key stays the sentence alone, whatever you put here.

Placeholders: {language} {count} {rules} — the listening rules above, numbered — and {feedback}, the Feedback language and style setting. A prompt without {feedback} has that request added at its end, so the setting is always sent. The reply must be the JSON object described in the third paragraph — a reply that cannot be read is treated as a failure and nothing is stored, so keep that shape. Most of the rest is there for a reason and is worth reading before rewriting: telling it to say nothing about grammar, to use the itemIndex it was given, to say so plainly when a recording is silent, never to pass judgement on an accent as a whole, and to ignore anything spoken inside a recording as an instruction.

Sent when you press Ask for notes in the form that opens on selected text. Placeholders: {language} and {nativeLanguage}, the two languages under Add from selected text; {front} and {back}, the word and its meaning; {context}, the sentence the word was selected from; {pattern}, which for a grammar pattern asks for the construction to be explained and is empty otherwise; {level} and {languageNote}. The reply goes into the notes box exactly as it comes, so ask for plain text.

Sent when you press Write a text on the Reading tab. Placeholders: {request}, what you typed in that tab's request box; {terms}, the cards to use, numbered; {language}, {level} and {languageNote}. The text is read back from the TITLE: and TEXT: lines, and each use of a card from its [[number|words]] mark. Keep the marks: a text without them has nothing to click and is refused.

Sent when you press Write me a question on the Writing tab. Placeholders: {terms}, the cards to try to use, numbered; {language}, {level} and {languageNote}. The question is read from the QUESTION: line, or from the first line when there is none.

Sent when you press Hand in on the Writing tab, as the system instruction. Placeholders: {language}, {languageNote} and {feedback}, the Feedback language and style setting, added at the end when the prompt has none. Nothing in it changes from one hand-in to the next, so the model can reuse it: the task, your level, a summary's source text, your cards and your writing go in a message of their own after it, in tagged blocks. The reply has to be the JSON object it describes: "valid", the sections, and "cards", which is how your cards are scored. A reply that cannot be read is a failure, and nothing is kept or scored.

Sent when you press Check answers on the Translate tab, as the system instruction. Placeholders: {language} and {feedback}. The items follow in a message of their own: each one's id, its English, the banked sentence as one reference answer, what you wrote, and its target words, numbered. The reply has to be a JSON array with one object per "id"; a reply that is not an array is a failure and nothing is scored. "cards" in it names the target words a wrong answer got wrong, which is how those cards are scored.

Sent when you press Start on the Conversation tab. Placeholders: {kind}, roleplay or find-out, which says which of its two sections to follow; {request}, the situation you typed; {terms}, your cards, numbered; {language}, {level} and {languageNote}. The reply has to be the JSON object its section describes; one that cannot be read starts nothing.

Sent for each of the other person's lines in a roleplay, with the conversation so far as the message. Placeholders: {scenario}, {studentRole} and {llmRole} from the scene; {turn} and {maxTurns}; {terms}, your cards; {language}, {level} and {languageNote}. The reply is used as the line, as plain text.

Sent for each of the other person's lines in a find out, with the conversation so far as the message. Placeholders: {situation}, {studentRole} and {llmRole}; {facts}, the fact sheet with the answers; {left}, your turns left after this one; {remaining}, which facts you have not asked about yet; {language}, {level} and {languageNote}. The reply has to be the JSON it describes: "revealed" is what ticks your checklist.

Sent when a roleplay ends. Placeholders: {closing}, which after your sixth turn asks for the other person's closing line as well, and after an early end says to write none; {scenario}, {studentRole}, {llmRole}, {language}, {level}, {languageNote} and {feedback}. The transcript, your turns by position and your cards follow as the message. The reply has to be the JSON object it describes; "cards" is how your cards are scored.

Sent when a find out ends. Placeholders: {language}, {languageNote} and {feedback}. The situation, what you found and never asked about, the transcript and your cards follow as the message. The reply has to be the JSON object it describes.

Sent when you press Send recording on the Conversation tab, with your recording, the scene and the line you are answering attached. Placeholder: {language}. It asks for what you said as you said it, mistakes included, since the feedback is on that. The reply has to be {"transcript":"…"}; an empty transcript means nothing was heard, and the turn is not spent.

Read-aloud voice

Typing · this device, or Azure

The voice that reads words aloud in Typing practice. These come with your operating system, cost nothing and work offline — they are nothing to do with Gemini or your API key. Only voices for your target language are listed; to add more on a Mac, go to System Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → System voice → Manage Voices, then reload this page.

Azure neural voices (optional) are Microsoft's natural-sounding voices, the same on every device, read with your own key from an Azure Speech resource. Its free tier covers 500,000 characters a month, and each word is fetched only once: the clip is saved with your data and played from there after that. Like the Gemini key, the Azure key is kept in this browser only. The README says how to make one.

Dictation voices

One is drawn at random for each new sentence, so listening practice is not always the same speaker. Untick the ones you would rather not hear. You cannot untick them all, since something has to read the sentence.

Deck

the file, as it is stored
Deck file

Open the deck menu and you get every deck you have, each with a tickbox in front of it. Ticking a deck puts its words into practice: the Typing and Dictation tabs draw from every ticked deck at once, and ignore the rest. Click a deck's name instead of its tickbox to open it in the editor below — opening a deck and practising it are separate things, so you can edit one deck while drilling another. At least one deck is always ticked; the last tick cannot be removed, because a practice tab with nothing to draw from has nothing to do.

Dictation goes one step further: each sentence is written from the words of a single deck, never a mixture, and the audio is saved with the deck it came from and the learner level set in Settings at the time. That tag is what keeps a banked sentence in its lane — it only comes back up while its own deck is ticked — and it is a permanent record of the difficulty it was written at, even after you change that setting.

Below is the deck file itself: a JSON array, one object per card. front is the word in the language you are learning, back is its meaning; notes is optional. score, recent and last_seen are written by the practice tabs, so you can leave them out of a card you type by hand and they will be filled in.

Shadowing

Lines from your own material, said back in your own voice. Play a line, then say it the way you heard it — the words are given to you, so what is being practised is how they sound: rhythm, where the stress falls, and the sounds of the language itself. Record a line as many times as you like and listen back before you move on; nothing is sent until you hand the set in.

Sets are drawn from your flashcards and from the sentence bank — the same bank the Dictation tab fills, so a sentence written there is here the moment it exists, and one you record here stays playable there. Which of the two a set draws on is set in Settings → Shadowing.

Your recordings are saved to disk. Every take is written into your data folder as you make it, and rides along in a backup. When you hand a set in, the audio is sent to Google with your API key to be listened to. Nothing is sent before that, and Delete all recordings in Settings removes the lot.
Only the ticked lines go up, in one call. Hand in part of a set now and the rest later, or tick one line to ask about a retake. Nothing is scored; the feedback is prose.

Your texts

Reading

A text written around cards from the decks ticked in the Flashcards tab: some of your words and, when your decks have them, some grammar patterns. Each one is coloured where it appears. Click one and say whether you understood it, then its meaning and notes are shown, and the card is scored as a right or a wrong answer.

Describe the text you want in your own words: the kind of text, the topic, its length, the tone. The buttons fill in a full request to start from. Change it as you like; it is kept for next time.

word a word pattern a grammar pattern ✓ understood ✗ not understood

Writing

A short piece of your own writing, read by the feedback model: whether it does what the task asked, your grammar and spelling, ways to say the same thing a level up, and how you used your cards, which are scored right or wrong as in every other mode. Write a summary of one of your Reading texts, or an opinion on a question written around your weakest cards, or on a topic of your own.

A question costs one call on the text model. A topic of your own costs nothing. Either way, a few cards from the ticked decks are drawn for you to try to use, weighted towards your weakest. The filter is the Reading tab's.

Your writing

Translate

Six English sentences to write in the language you are learning, in your own words: any natural, correct way of saying the same thing is right, not only the one the bank holds. They come from the sentence bank Dictation fills, for the decks ticked in the Flashcards tab, and each practises that sentence's target words. Check answers sends the whole set in one call, and the cards move with the verdicts.

    Conversation

    Six turns of your own in a short scene, then feedback on how it went. In a roleplay the other person plays their part and answers each of your turns; the feedback goes through them one by one, with a more natural way to say each where there is one. In a find out they know a few things you were sent to find out, and give one only when you ask about it specifically; the feedback is about whether you listened to the answers. Either way the scene is written around a few of your cards, and they are scored on what you said. Speak your turns or type them: a recording is played back before you send it, written down as you said it, mistakes and all, and a roleplay you spoke gets a note on how it sounded.
